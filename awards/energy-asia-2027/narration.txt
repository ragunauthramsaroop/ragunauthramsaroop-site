At the Aurora Gold Mine in Guyana, reliable power is not optional. Every hour, electricity sustains mineral processing, essential services, and the people who keep the operation running.

For years, this remote mine depended on diesel generation and fuel transported over long distances. AGM Incorporated saw an opportunity to reduce that dependence without sacrificing energy security.

The response was a phased hybrid power system integrating solar generation, battery storage, and existing dispatchable power. In April 2026, Aurora commissioned its second major solar expansion, bringing publicly reported installed solar capacity to 49.4 megawatts. Phase Two alone added 31.4 megawatts of solar and 60 megawatt hours of battery storage.

The integrated system is designed to serve all electricity demand in the mine's living quarters and approximately 75 percent of the concentrator plant's electricity needs. Aurora has moved from a diesel-only model to an operating hybrid energy system.

The expected annual benefits are substantial: approximately 63 gigawatt hours of clean electricity, 18 million litres of diesel displaced, and 47,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions avoided. These are published annual projections, not verified full-year operating results. Corporate reporting has already confirmed reduced diesel dependence and lower production costs.

Aurora's innovation is not a single piece of equipment. It is the way proven technologies, phased investment, reliable backup generation, and operational planning work together at an isolated industrial site.

The project also created approximately 100 local construction jobs and renewable-energy training opportunities. Its value reaches beyond the mine, supporting skills, economic participation, and Guyana's low-carbon development priorities.

There is a wider lesson for Asia. Mines and industrial facilities with continuous electricity demand face similar energy-security challenges. The Aurora approach starts with the actual load, develops solar and storage in stages, and protects reliability throughout the transition.

Future phases are planned to expand renewable generation and storage for Aurora's evolving operations. The model is designed for adaptation, not simple duplication. Different sites require different engineering, financing, and climate-resilience choices.

Aurora shows what industrial decarbonisation looks like when ambition becomes operating infrastructure. Less dependence on diesel. A stronger energy system. A practical pathway for remote industries to advance toward a more sustainable future.
